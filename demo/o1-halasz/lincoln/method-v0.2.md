# Lincoln method for an O-1A packet, version 0.2 (2026-11-10)

Written after case 2 and its answer key were committed (commit c64d99f). Changes from v0.1 come from
the case 1 judges' findings (thin legal frame, missing forms, internal row IDs left in the outputs) and
from standard intake practice for a real client file. Written for any O-1A case. Four messages; send
each only after the previous reply has finished. Attach the client's files to message 1.

## Message 1: intake and extraction

You are preparing the evidence for an O-1A petition for a US immigration attorney, who will review and
sign everything. You do not give legal advice and you never invent facts. Work in four passes over the
next four messages. This message is pass 1: intake and extraction only. Do not argue eligibility yet.

The client's files are attached. Real client files are messy: read every page, including scanned pages
that are images only (read the image itself), spreadsheets, and documents in other languages.

Step 1. Document inventory. One row per document: a short title, the page range in the file, date,
author or issuer, language, whether it is a scan, whether it is signed, final or a draft, and whether it
duplicates or supersedes another document.

Step 2. Facts table. One row per fact: the fact in neutral words, an exact quote (for a scan, transcribe
it), document title and page, the document's date, who is speaking and their relationship to the
beneficiary and the petitioner (independent, customer or partner, employer or colleague, supervisor or
co-author, advisory board or shareholder, the beneficiary or her family, unknown), and the type of source
(government, peer reviewed journal, professional or trade publication with stated circulation, general
or regional newspaper, company document, self published, other).

Step 3. Red flags. List every case of:
* the same fact given differently in different documents (titles, money, counts, dates, names), saying
  which source is later, signed, independent or otherwise more reliable, and which figure to use;
* recognition that could be bought or self nominated (fees, packages, invoices, open nominations);
* journals or events that invite anyone, cover every field, or charge authors with little review;
* drafts, unsigned or superseded versions, and duplicates;
* claims made only by the beneficiary or her employer;
* statements written by the beneficiary rather than about her;
* documents not in English and without a certified translation;
* different spellings or versions of the beneficiary's name;
* dates that affect filing: passport expiry, current status and I-94 expiry, requested start date.

## Message 2: assess the criteria and filing requirements

Pass 2: assess eligibility using only the pass 1 tables.

Legal frame: 8 CFR 214.2(o)(3)(iii). Either a one time major internationally recognised award under
(o)(3)(iii)(A), or at least three of the eight criteria in (o)(3)(iii)(B)(1) to (8), or comparable
evidence under (o)(3)(iii)(C) if a criterion does not readily apply. Then a final merits review of all
the evidence together (USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 2, Part M): does it show sustained national or
international acclaim and that the beneficiary is among the small percentage at the very top of the
field, on a preponderance of the evidence.

Apply each criterion's own standard strictly, and give its citation:
(B)(1) Prizes or awards: nationally or internationally recognised, for excellence in the field. Internal,
  local or purchasable awards do not qualify. Note the selection process and number of nominees.
(B)(2) Membership: the association must require outstanding achievements, judged by recognised national
  or international experts. Read the association's own rules; a higher grade such as Fellow can qualify
  where ordinary membership does not. Open or paid membership does not qualify.
(B)(3) Published material about the beneficiary and her work, in professional or major trade
  publications or major media, with title, date and author. Pieces written by the beneficiary, by her
  employer, or paid for do not count here. Circulation or readership evidence matters.
(B)(4) Judging the work of others in the same or an allied field. Selective journals and conferences are
  strong; predatory journals and local events are not.
(B)(5) Original contributions of major significance: impact beyond her employer (adoption by others,
  citations, independent letters with specifics, measured results). Pending patents alone are not enough.
(B)(6) Scholarly articles in professional journals or major media.
(B)(7) Critical or essential role for organisations with a distinguished reputation: both the role and
  the reputation must be shown; employer statements alone are weak.
(B)(8) High salary: compare the pay for the O-1 role with reliable data for the same title, field and
  location. At or below the median does not meet it.

For each: claim, do not claim, or claim only with more evidence; supporting documents with pages; main
weakness; strength (strong, moderate, weak). Weigh letters by independence and disclose any relationship.
For co-authored work, use the author contribution statements to say exactly what the beneficiary did.

Then check the filing requirements and mark each present, missing, inadequate or unclear:
* written advisory opinion (consultation) from an appropriate peer group, labour or management
  organisation, 8 CFR 214.2(o)(2)(ii)(D) and (o)(5); a letter from someone connected to the petitioner
  is not a peer group consultation;
* copy of the contract, or a summary of an oral agreement, (o)(2)(ii)(B);
* explanation of the events or activities with dates, and an itinerary where needed, (o)(2)(ii)(C);
* certified English translations of every foreign language document, 8 CFR 103.2(b)(3);
* Form I-129 with the O supplement, filing fee, Form G-28;
* passport valid for the requested period, current status and I-94 expiry, and the filing deadline that
  follows from them;
* evidence that the beneficiary's name is consistent across documents, or an explanation.

## Message 3: draft

Pass 3: draft the four documents, using only the pass 1 facts and the pass 2 conclusions.

1. Petition support letter from the petitioner to USCIS. Structure: introduction; the beneficiary and
   the field; the legal standard with regulation citations; one section per claimed criterion, each
   headed with its citation; the consultation; the final merits argument; conclusion. Every factual
   sentence ends with a citation to the document title and page in the client file. Use the reliable
   figure for every conflict and never repeat a superseded, draft or inflated one. Do not use excluded
   evidence. Do not write caveats addressed to USCIS; put notes for the attorney in [square brackets].
   Never say a document is enclosed if it has not been obtained; write [to be obtained].
2. Criteria map table: criterion and citation, recommendation, evidence with pages, weakness, strength.
3. Exhibit index in filing order: forms, required documents, evidence by criterion, supporting
   material. List excluded documents separately with the reason. Mark items still to be obtained.
4. Gaps and risks: every missing or inadequate requirement, every weak criterion, every conflict, every
   date that affects filing, with the action for the attorney.

Do not use internal row numbers or IDs from earlier passes in these documents.

## Message 4: verify and finalise

Pass 4: review your own work as a strict reviewer who did not write it.

1. Check the letter sentence by sentence: the cited page contains the fact, the figure is the reliable
   one, nothing comes from a draft, duplicate or excluded document, and nothing is overstated.
2. Check every criterion claim against its regulation standard and that no excluded evidence is used.
3. Check that every red flag from pass 1 is resolved in the letter or listed in the gaps.
4. Check the filing requirements list is complete, including dates.
5. Fix everything, then output the final versions of all four documents, followed by a short list of
   the corrections you made in this pass.
