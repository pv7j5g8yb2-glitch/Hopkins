The legal standard
Section 101(a)(15)(O)(i) of the Immigration and Nationality Act provides a nonimmigrant
classification for a person who has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education,
business or athletics, demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim (8
U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(O)(i)). The O-1A classification covers extraordinary ability in the
sciences, education, business or athletics (8 CFR 214.2(o)(1)(ii)(A)(1)). Extraordinary
ability in the field of science, education, business or athletics means "a level of expertise
indicating that the person is one of the small percentage who have arisen to the very top
of the field of endeavor" (8 CFR 214.2(o)(3)(ii)).
A petitioner may establish eligibility in one of two ways. The first is evidence of a major,
internationally recognised award (8 CFR 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(A)). The second is evidence
meeting at least three of the eight criteria (8 CFR 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(B)(1) to (8)). Where a
criterion does not readily apply to the occupation, comparable evidence may be
submitted (8 CFR 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(C)).
USCIS reviews the evidence in two steps (USCIS Policy Manual, Vol. 2, Part M, Ch. 4).
[Attorney: confirm the current Policy Manual section and the 8 CFR 214.2(o)(6)(iii) pin
cite.] It first decides whether the evidence meets the regulatory criteria. It then reviews
all the evidence together in a final merits determination, to decide whether the
beneficiary has sustained national or international acclaim and is among the small
percentage at the very top of the field (USCIS Policy Manual, Vol. 2, Part M, Ch. 4). The
standard of proof is a preponderance of the evidence (Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec.
369, 375–76 (AAO 2010)).
The petition must also include a written advisory opinion from an appropriate peer group,
a copy of any written contract or a summary of an oral agreement, and an explanation of
the events or activities, with an itinerary where needed (8 CFR 214.2(o)(2)(ii)(B) to (D); 8
CFR 214.2(o)(5)).

supervision and funding (Letter of Prof. Elena Marchetti, client file p. 29).

E. Original scientific or scholarly contributions of major significance
The Beneficiary conceived FoldSight and wrote the software, according to the author-contribution statement in the 2022 peer-
reviewed FoldSight paper. That paper describes a graph neural network trained on 14,000 molecular-dynamics trajectories 
covering 2,900 proteins, designed to predict the range of shapes a protein binding pocket can take from a static structure. On 
a held-out set of 310 proteins, the paper reports recovery of 83% of cryptic pockets, compared with 41% for the prior best 
method, with a median run time of 4.2 minutes per protein. The paper states that the software was released as open source. 
(Client PDF, pp. 33-35.)
The record also includes independent expert evidence of adoption and downstream use. Professor Lucia Ferrante, a 
computational-biology professor who states that she has never worked with the Beneficiary, Corvane, or the Beneficiary’s 
former laboratory, reports using FoldSight in her own group. She states that her group and at least four other groups known 
to her use it routinely, and that several pharmaceutical companies use it. She describes the method as enabling prediction of 
binding-pocket shape changes in minutes rather than the weeks of simulation previously required. (Client PDF, p. 27.)
Dr. Mark Delaney, Vice President of Computational Chemistry at Northgate Pharma, states that his team began using 
FoldSight in 2023, before Northgate’s relationship with Corvane. He reports that the team identified a previously unknown 
binding site in an oncology target using FoldSight, that a candidate drug designed against the site entered a Phase 1 clinical 
trial in 2026, and that FoldSight has been used in three Northgate discovery programmes. He also states that Northgate’s 
2025 paid research collaboration with Corvane was partly because of this work. (Client PDF, p. 28.)
The later peer-reviewed kinome study reports a screen across all 518 human kinases, identifying 92 previously unreported 
binding sites, 17 of which were confirmed by crystallography. Its author-contribution statement attributes to the Beneficiary 
the adaptation of FoldSight for kinases and analysis of pocket-motion results. (Client PDF, pp. 36-38.)
A separate 2024 benchmark paper compares seven pocket-flexibility predictors on 1,200 approved and clinical drug targets. 
It states that FoldSight and two newer methods built on it performed best; its author-contribution statement says the 
Beneficiary designed the benchmark and wrote the paper. (Client PDF, pp. 39-40.)

citations to the FoldSight paper. These are the dated figures in the packet; the Petitioner does not use the higher,

5. Legal and regulatory references for the filing
8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(1)(ii)(A)(1): O-1 classification applies to an individual with extraordinary ability in the sciences 
demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim who is coming temporarily to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability. Under § 214.2(o)(3)(ii), extraordinary ability in science means a level of expertise indicating that the 
individual is among the small percentage at the very top of the field.
8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(A)-(B): one-time major internationally recognized award route or at least three of the eight listed 
criteria; the eight criteria are the basis for the map above.
8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(2)(ii)(B)-(D): written contract (or oral terms summary), activity explanation/itinerary, and written advisory 
opinion. Section 214.2(o)(2)(iv)(A): itinerary with dates and locations when work requires multiple locations.
8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(5)(i)-(ii): required peer-group/person-with-expertise consultation; for a favorable O-1A advisory opinion, 
the regulation says the opinion should describe ability and achievements, the nature of duties, and whether the position 
requires services of an individual of extraordinary ability.
8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(3): any foreign-language document submitted to USCIS must include a full English translation certified as 
complete and accurate, together with the translator’s certification of competence.
