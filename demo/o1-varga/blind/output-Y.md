Dear Officer,
Tidewell Labs, Inc. ("Tidewell") submits this letter in support of its petition to classify Lena Varga in O-1A status as an individual of extraordinary ability in the sciences. Tidewell seeks to employ Ms Varga as its Chief Technology Officer for three years, from 1 March 2027 to 28 February 2030 (Ex. B, p. 1). As set out below, Ms Varga meets five of the eight criteria at 8 CFR 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(B), and the evidence as a whole shows sustained national acclaim.
The petitioner
Tidewell is a Delaware corporation based in Austin, Texas (Ex. B, p. 1). It builds software that predicts failures in grid-scale battery storage systems (Ex. A, p. 1). Its customers include Cascade Valley Electric in Oregon, Pinecrest Municipal Utility in Arizona and Bayline Power Cooperative in Georgia (Ex. B, p. 2). Tidewell closed its Series A financing on 30 September 2025, raising USD 11,500,000 at final close (Ex. L, p. 2). It had 31 employees as of 30 September 2025 (Ex. L, p. 2).
The beneficiary
Ms Varga is a citizen of Hungary, born in Budapest (Ex. A, p. 1). She holds a BSc (2016) and an MSc (2018) in Electrical Engineering from Danube Technical University, and her MSc thesis was titled "Early detection of capacity drift in stationary lithium cells" (Ex. A, p. 1). From 2016 to 2019 she was a Research Assistant at the university's Energy Systems Lab (Ex. A, p. 1). From June 2019 to December 2023 she was Lead Engineer, Battery Analytics, at Northfield Power Systems, where she led a team of seven engineers (Ex. L, p. 3). Since January 2024 she has been co-founder and Chief Technology Officer of Tidewell (Ex. A, p. 1). She is a named inventor, with Klaus Brandt, on US Patent Application 18/552,904, published on 6 February 2025 and pending examination (Ex. M, p. 1).
The proposed employment
Ms Varga will serve as Chief Technology Officer at a base salary of USD 210,000 per year, with options over 4.0 percent of Tidewell's fully diluted shares vesting over four years (Ex. B, p. 1). She will lead all engineering and research, own the fault prediction platform, manage a team of eleven engineers, and present to utility customers and investors (Ex. B, p. 1). She will work in Austin and travel quarterly for on-site deployment reviews with the three customer utilities named above (Ex. B, pp. 1–2). The itinerary also includes the 2027 launch of version 3 of the platform for sodium ion battery sites and a planned annual presentation at the International Conference on Energy Storage Analytics (Ex. B, p. 2).
Criterion 1: nationally recognised prize for excellence in the field
On 18 September 2025, the American Grid Storage Council told Ms Varga that she had been selected as one of two recipients of its 2025 National Energy Innovation Prize, in recognition of her drift-based fault prediction method for grid-scale batteries (Ex. C, p. 1). The prize carries an award of USD 50,000 (Ex. C, p. 1). The Council describes itself as the national trade association for the energy storage industry, with more than 900 member companies (Ex. C, p. 2). The 2025 prize drew 340 nominations from 31 states (Ex. C, p. 2). According to the Council, nominees were judged by an independent panel of nine experts from national laboratories, universities and utilities, on originality, measured impact in the field and adoption by industry (Ex. C, p. 2). The Council recognised Ms Varga for a method that predicts battery cell failures weeks in advance by tracking small drifts in capacity (Ex. C, p. 2). The trade publication Grid Systems Weekly also reported the award (Ex. E, p. 2). The prize is national in scope, is chosen by an expert panel from 340 nominations, and recognises originality, measured impact and industry adoption in grid energy storage, Ms Varga's field (Ex. C, pp. 1–2).
Criterion 3: published material about the beneficiary
On 12 March 2026, Grid Systems Weekly published a feature article about Ms Varga and her work, titled "THE ENGINEER TEACHING BATTERIES TO ASK FOR HELP", by Senior Editor Marcus Hale (Ex. E, p. 1). The article describes her study of the small capacity drifts that appear weeks before a battery cell fails, and her drift model, first published in 2023 (Ex. E, p. 1). It quotes Ms Varga, and quotes a vice president of grid operations at Cascade Valley Electric on how the system changed his team's maintenance planning (Ex. E, p. 1). According to the publication's 2026 media kit, Grid Systems Weekly has been published since 1998 and covers power grids and energy storage for engineers and utility executives (Ex. E, p. 2). The media kit states a print and digital circulation of 48,000, a readership mainly of utility managers, grid engineers and energy investors in North America, and a full-time editorial staff of 22 (Ex. E, p. 2). The article is about Ms Varga and her work, names its author and date, and appears in a professional trade publication in her field (Ex. E, pp. 1–2).
Criterion 4: judging the work of others
Ms Varga served on the program committee of the International Conference on Energy Storage Analytics (ICESA) 2025 (Ex. H, p. 1). The Program Chair, Professor Anil Mehta, confirms that she completed peer reviews of 14 submitted research papers between 10 February and 20 March 2025 (Ex. H, p. 1). ICESA 2025 received 412 submissions and accepted 118, an acceptance rate of 29 percent (Ex. H, p. 1). Program committee members are invited on the basis of their published research in the field (Ex. H, p. 1). The papers she reviewed were submissions to a conference on energy storage analytics, the field of her own published work (Ex. H, p. 1; Ex. I, pp. 1–2).
Criterion 5: original contributions of major significance
Ms Varga's contribution is a drift model for early fault prediction in grid storage, first published in 2023 (Ex. I, p. 1). The 2023 article reported that small, steady drifts in measured cell capacity precede most cell failures in stationary lithium iron phosphate systems by three to six weeks (Ex. I, p. 1). Using data from 4,200 cells, the authors report a model that flags at-risk cells with 91 percent precision (Ex. I, p. 1). A 2024 field validation across 19 utility sites and 26 months reported that drift-based prediction gave operators a median of 24 days' warning before cell failure and reduced unplanned outages by 37 percent (Ex. I, p. 2).
Independent expert assessment. Professor Ruth Calloway, Chair of the Department of Electrical Engineering at Midlands State Institute of Technology, has 25 years of research on battery reliability and more than 140 published papers (Ex. J, p. 1). She states that she has never worked with Ms Varga or Tidewell, and knows her work only through her publications and its use in the field (Ex. J, p. 1). She explains that before the drift model, most monitoring waited for a cell to show clear damage, and that Ms Varga's work showed warning signs appear weeks earlier and gave a practical way to detect them (Ex. J, p. 1). Professor Calloway states that she teaches the drift model in her graduate course, and that at least three research groups she knows of have built on it (Ex. J, p. 1). In her view it is "one of the most important contributions to grid storage reliability of the last decade" (Ex. J, p. 1). The Council's prize panel judged nominees on measured impact and adoption by industry, and the Council noted that the method is now used by several US utilities (Ex. C, p. 2).
Citations. As of 1 November 2026, a scholarly search engine records 212 citations of the 2023 article and 97 citations of the 2024 article (Ex. I, p. 3).
Adoption and measured results. Cascade Valley Electric, which serves 1.1 million customers, has used Tidewell's platform, built on Ms Varga's drift model, across 41 of its battery storage sites since 2024 (Ex. J, p. 2). Cascade Valley reports that unplanned battery outages at those sites fell by 37 percent in the 18 months after deployment, compared with the 18 months before (Ex. J, p. 2). Its vice president states that Cascade Valley paid Tidewell as a customer and that he has no other relationship with Ms Varga (Ex. J, p. 2). Her former employer, Northfield Power Systems, states that her drift detection work was built into its monitoring product, which is installed at storage sites operated by its utility customers (Ex. L, p. 3).
Together, the independent expert's account, the citation record, the prize panel's assessment and adoption by utilities show that the contribution has had major significance in the field beyond Tidewell (Ex. C, p. 2; Ex. I, p. 3; Ex. J, pp. 1–2; Ex. L, p. 3).
Criterion 6: authorship of scholarly articles
Ms Varga is the first author of two articles in the Journal of Energy Storage Analytics, a peer-reviewed journal published since 2010 (Ex. I, pp. 1–2). The journal's listed 2025 impact factor is 6.8 (Ex. I, p. 1). [Attorney: confirm the source of this figure or remove it; see Gaps #14.] The first article is "A drift model for early fault prediction in grid storage", with Klaus Brandt, in volume 14, issue 2 (2023), pages 101 to 118 (Ex. As of 1 November 2026, the two articles had been cited 212 and 97 times respectively (Ex. I, p. 3).
Final merits: sustained national acclaim
Taken together, the evidence shows that Ms Varga has sustained national acclaim and is among the small percentage at the very top of her field.
The acclaim is sustained. It runs from her 2023 article (Ex. I, p. 1), through the 2024 field validation (Ex. I, p. 2), her ICESA peer reviews in early 2025 (Ex. H, p. 1), the national prize in September 2025 (Ex. C, p. 1) and the trade press feature in March 2026 (Ex. E, p. 1), to citations recorded on 1 November 2026 (Ex. I, p. 3) and an independent expert's assessment in October 2026 (Ex. J, p. 1).
The acclaim comes from independent sources. They include a national trade association's expert panel (Ex. C, p. 2), a peer-reviewed journal (Ex. I, pp. 1–2), a conference program committee that invites members for their published research (Ex. H, p. 1), and a trade publication with a stated circulation of 48,000 (Ex. E, p. 2). They also include a professor who has never worked with her (Ex. J, p. 1). In addition, a utility that pays Tidewell as a customer reports measured results at its own sites (Ex. J, p. 2).
The evidence places her at the top of the field. She was one of two recipients of a national prize that drew 340 nominations (Ex. C, pp. 1–2). As of 1 November 2026, her 2023 article had been cited 212 times (Ex. I, p. 3). An independent expert with 25 years in battery reliability calls her model one of the most important contributions to grid storage reliability of the last decade (Ex. J, p. 1). She served on a program committee whose members are invited on the basis of their published research (Ex. H, p. 1).
For these reasons, Tidewell respectfully submits that Ms Varga meets the regulatory criteria and, on a preponderance of the evidence, qualifies for O-1A classification. Tidewell asks that the petition be approved.
