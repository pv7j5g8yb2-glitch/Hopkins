III. Legal standard
Extraordinary ability in the sciences, education, business or athletics means “a level of expertise indicating that the person is one of the small percentage who have arisen to the very top of the field of endeavor” (8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(ii)). The beneficiary must demonstrate sustained national or international acclaim and recognition for achievements in the field (8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)). She may do so through a major, internationally recognized award, or through evidence meeting at least three of the eight criteria (8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(A)–(B)). USCIS first decides which criteria the evidence meets. It then weighs the evidence as a whole to decide whether the beneficiary has sustained acclaim and is among the small percentage at the very top of the field (2 USCIS-PM M.4). The standard of proof is a preponderance of the evidence.
Ms Varga's field is grid-scale energy storage engineering, specifically battery health analytics and failure prediction.
