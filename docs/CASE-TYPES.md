# Every kind of work a US immigration firm does (2026-11-12)

From general knowledge of US immigration practice, checked against form numbers USCIS uses. The
immigration lawyer adviser should confirm it. Policies change often (fees, lottery rules, parole
programmes, DACA), so each library entry records the date and edition it was checked against.
Lincoln's library has one entry per item below, grouped into the families at the end.

## 1. Family based
* Relative petitions (I-130, I-130A): spouses, parents, children, siblings; immediate relatives and
  preference categories F1, F2A, F2B, F3, F4; Child Status Protection Act age calculations.
* Fiancé visas (I-129F for K-1, with K-2 children; K-3 rarely).
* Green card applications in the US (I-485 with I-864 or I-864A affidavit of support, I-693 medical,
  I-765 work permit, I-131 advance parole).
* Consular processing abroad (National Visa Center, DS-260, civil documents, interview preparation).
* Removing conditions on a marriage green card (I-751, joint or with a waiver).
* Widow or widower petitions (I-360); adoption cases (I-600, I-600A, I-800, I-800A).
* Follow to join for spouses and children; action on an approved petition (I-824).

## 2. Work visas (temporary)
* H-1B: lottery registration, cap and cap exempt petitions, transfers, extensions, amendments, labour
  condition applications (ETA-9035); H-1B1 (Chile, Singapore); E-3 (Australia); H-4 and H-4 work permits.
* L-1A and L-1B, blanket L, new office L-1.
* O-1A, O-1B, O-2, O-3; P-1, P-2, P-3.
* E-1 and E-2 treaty traders and investors, and their employees.
* TN (Canada, Mexico); H-2A and H-2B seasonal workers (ETA-9142); H-3 trainees; Q-1; R-1 religious
  workers; I visas for media.
* J-1 exchange visitors and two year home residency waivers (I-612, Conrad 30, no objection letters).
* Students: F-1 and M-1 changes of status, OPT and STEM OPT work permits, reinstatement, cap gap.
* Visitors: B-1 and B-2 visas, extensions and changes of status (I-539).
* International Entrepreneur Parole (I-941).

## 3. Green cards through work or investment
* PERM labour certification: prevailing wage (ETA-9141), recruitment, ETA-9089, audits, supervised
  recruitment, appeals to the Board of Alien Labor Certification Appeals.
* Employer and self petitions (I-140): EB-1A, EB-1B, EB-1C, EB-2, EB-2 National Interest Waiver,
  EB-3, Schedule A (nurses, physical therapists), physician waivers.
* Special immigrants (I-360): religious workers, Afghan and Iraqi special immigrant visas, others.
* Investors (EB-5): I-526E, regional centre or direct, removing conditions (I-829).
* Green card stage: I-485 with job portability (Supplement J), priority dates and the monthly Visa
  Bulletin, concurrent filing, consular processing.

## 4. Employer compliance
* I-9 and E-Verify audits, ICE notices of inspection, fines and settlements.
* Public access files, Department of Labor wage investigations, USCIS site visits.
* Mergers and acquisitions due diligence, successor in interest, layoffs and grace periods.
* Business travel advice and global mobility programmes (other countries through partner firms).

## 5. Humanitarian
* Asylum (I-589): affirmative and in court; withholding of removal; Convention Against Torture.
* Refugee and asylee relatives (I-730).
* TPS (I-821) and re-registration; DACA renewals (I-821D).
* U visas for crime victims (I-918, Supplement B certification, I-929, later green cards).
* T visas for trafficking victims (I-914); VAWA self petitions (I-360).
* Special Immigrant Juvenile Status (I-360, with a state court order).
* Parole: humanitarian (I-131), parole in place for military families, parole programmes.
* Other: Cuban Adjustment Act, NACARA, deferred action requests, credible and reasonable fear
  interviews.

## 6. Citizenship and keeping status
* Naturalisation (N-400) with fee waivers (I-912), disability exceptions (N-648), military
  naturalisation (N-426), appeals (N-336); interview preparation.
* Certificates of citizenship (N-600, N-600K); replacements (N-565); derivation and acquisition analysis.
* Green card renewal or replacement (I-90); re-entry permits and refugee travel documents (I-131);
  preserving residence (N-470); returning resident visas (SB-1); giving up residence (I-407).
* Record fixes: I-94 corrections (I-102, CBP deferred inspection), name and date errors.

## 7. Waivers
* Inadmissibility waivers (I-601), provisional unlawful presence waivers (I-601A), permission to
  reapply (I-212), 212(h) and 212(i) waivers, nonimmigrant waivers (212(d)(3)).

## 8. Responses, motions and appeals
* Requests for Evidence, Notices of Intent to Deny, Revoke or Terminate.
* Motions and appeals to USCIS's Administrative Appeals Office (I-290B).
* Expedite requests, premium processing (I-907), USCIS Ombudsman requests, congressional enquiries.
* Visa refusals at consulates (221(g) administrative processing, 214(b)), revocations.
* Border problems: entry refusals, expedited removal, CBP record issues.

## 9. Immigration court (removal defence)
* Master and individual hearings, pleadings, evidence packets, country conditions research.
* Relief: cancellation of removal for residents (EOIR-42A) and non residents (EOIR-42B), adjustment
  in court, asylum and related relief, waivers, voluntary departure, prosecutorial discretion,
  termination or administrative closure.
* Bond, detention and parole requests; stays of removal (I-246); ICE check ins.
* Motions to reopen or reconsider (including orders made in absence); appeals to the Board of
  Immigration Appeals (EOIR-26) with briefs.
* Advice to criminal defence lawyers on immigration consequences.

## 10. Federal court
* Petitions for review in the courts of appeals, habeas corpus, lawsuits over delayed cases
  (mandamus and Administrative Procedure Act).

## 11. Records
* FOIA requests to USCIS, CBP, ICE, EOIR and the State Department; FBI identity history checks.

## 12. Running the practice
* Enquiries, consultations and eligibility screening; conflict checks; engagement letters; flat fee
  billing and trust accounting; calendars and deadlines; client updates in other languages;
  translations; document collection; fees, filing and online accounts; receipts; biometrics; interview
  preparation; case status; Visa Bulletin; policy changes.

## Families Lincoln's library is built on
Case types in a family share the same structure, so each family gets one deep test case.
1. Petitions proved by evidence (O, P, EB-1, NIW, L, E, H-1B, R, Q).
2. Relationship petitions (I-130, K-1, I-751, widow, VAWA).
3. Green card applications (I-485, consular processing, EB-5 stages).
4. Labour certification (PERM).
5. Humanitarian applications (asylum, U, T, SIJS, TPS, DACA, parole).
6. Citizenship and documents (N-400, N-600, I-90, travel documents, record fixes).
7. Waivers.
8. Responses and appeals (RFE, NOID, I-290B, consular refusals).
9. Immigration court preparation (applications, briefs, motions, BIA appeals).
10. Federal court drafting (petitions for review, habeas, mandamus).
11. Employer compliance (I-9, public access files, audits).
12. FOIA and records.
Lincoln prepares everything for court work, but a lawyer must appear in court.
