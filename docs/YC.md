# Y Combinator: what others are building (read 2026-11-04)

Source: the YC startup directory and every Request for Startups from Summer 2024 to Winter 2027,
pasted by Ismael on 2026-11-04. Only what that text says is recorded here. The company pages for
Grocalo and Remix were fetched the same day and showed nothing beyond their one line (the pages
draw in the browser), so nothing more is known about them yet.

## The numbers on the directory

* "Since 2005, we have invested in over 5,000 companies that have a combined valuation of over $1T."
* 6,365 companies listed. 91 are marked Top Companies, about 1.4%. That's Ismael's point: very few
  make it big.
* Recent batches: Fall 2025 145, Winter 2026 197, Spring 2026 193, Summer 2026 228, Fall 2026 204,
  Winter 2027 8, Summer 2027 1. YC now runs four batches a year.
* By industry: B2B 3,230, Consumer 891, Healthcare 709, Fintech 669, Industrials 510, Real Estate
  and Construction 166 (Housing and Real Estate 86, Construction 52), Education 126.
* Real estate is a small corner of YC: 86 housing and real estate companies in 20 years.

## What the recent batches are mostly building

From reading every one line in the list (2025 to 2027 batches):

1. Tools and infrastructure for AI agents: sandboxes, cloud computers, memory, evals, RL
   environments, monitoring, payments and security for agents. The biggest single group.
2. "AI-native" firms that do the work themselves instead of selling software: law firms (Wingman
   Law, General Legal, Moritz, LegalOS), accounting (Bear, Billow, Rational "zero-human"), insurance
   brokerages (Casey, Fernstone, Panta, Kinro), tax (ClaimKit), audit (Arden, Denki), freight
   brokerages (Peer, LinkLane), a commercial real estate brokerage (Obia), an appraisal firm
   (Automax.ai).
3. Defence and drones, robotics and physical AI, energy and data centres, space.
4. Vertical "operating systems" run by agents: auto shops (CarSignal, Parrot), hotels (Axelrod,
   Lance, Zaplar), dental practices (Kairos Health, Patientdesk.ai), home services (Robby,
   CentralComs), restaurants (Marble), real estate brokerages (RealPact).

Marketing is a small share. Consumer content is smaller still.

## What YC's partners say they want (Requests for Startups) that bears on Hopkins

* **Vertical AI agents** (Spring 2025, Jared Friedman): SaaS made workers "incrementally more
  efficient"; vertical agents "automate the work entirely", and "vertical AI agents that reach
  human-level performance grow extremely quickly". "Possible this opportunity is big enough to
  mint another 100 unicorns."
* **Full-stack AI companies** (Summer 2025): don't sell the tool to the firm, become the firm.
* **AI-native agencies** (Spring 2026, Aaron Epstein): agencies were hard to scale; now "instead of
  selling software to customers to help them do the work, you can charge way more by using the
  software yourself and selling them the finished product". Names "an ad agency that uses AI to
  create stunning video ads". "Agencies of the future will look more like software companies,
  with software margins."
* **AI-native service companies** (Summer 2026, Gustaf Alströmer): "don't sell software, they sell
  the service". "The total spend on services is many times larger than the spend on software."
* **AI personal staff for everyone** (Spring 2025, David Lieb) and **AI personal assistant**
  (Summer 2025, Tom Blomfield): bring to everyone what only the rich could hire; go from "to-do"
  lists to "done" lists.
* **The first 10-person, $100B company** (Fall 2025): optimise for revenue per employee.
* **SaaS challengers** (Summer 2026): AI-native from the ground up, or bundle many point tools
  into one, instead of "a chatbot bolted onto a 2010 UI".
* **LLMs that write well** (Winter 2027, Jared Friedman): today's models "sound more robotic" than
  2024 and 2025's, because the rewards that make them good at code "flatten voice and style".
  Matters for every caption Hopkins writes.
* **More design founders** (Summer 2025): as building gets easy, design and taste matter more.
* **Company brain** (Summer 2026): agents need the business's know-how in one living place.
* **Video generation as a primitive** (Fall 2025): mentions listings auto-staged with your own
  furniture. Hopkins never uses AI generated images, so this one is out for us.
* **The $15-an-hour expert** (Winter 2027), **multiplayer AI** (Fall 2026), **consumer AI for a
  billion people** (Fall 2026), **proving you're human** (Fall 2026): general trends, not Hopkins.

## What it means for Hopkins (Claude's read, 2026-11-04)

* Running social content with AI is a crowded idea: at least a dozen funded teams do some version
  for creators, brands or small businesses. A general version would lose. The vertical (real
  estate agents, their city, their listings, Fair Housing, the Monday approval) is the edge, and
  nobody in this list holds it.
* YC's own partners are betting on doing the work end to end and selling the result, which is what
  the pivot already is. That sits uneasily with the Hopkins rule against "done for you" wording;
  the rule is about how it sounds, the model is about what it does.
* Scale, from Hopkins' own prices: at $99 a month an agent is about $1,190 a year, so $10M a year
  takes about 8,400 paying agents and $100M about 84,000. At C&C's $74, $10M takes about 11,300.
  Single agents alone make an 8 figure company possible; 9 figures likely needs teams,
  brokerages (RealPact's lane) and later the ads layer (Lapis's and Adside's lane) on top.
