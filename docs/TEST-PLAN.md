# Three week test plan (draft, 2026-11-09)

The point is to find out, cheaply, whether immigration lawyers would use and pay for this,
before spending months building. Nothing here needs client files. Nothing here claims a feature
that does not exist.

## Before anything else

* A parent or guardian agrees to be the legal owner and payment holder. Nothing is sold or
  signed without them.
* No LinkedIn account that pretends to be someone else. LinkedIn's minimum age is believed to be
  16 (verify). Use the parent's or an honest advisor's account, or email and other platforms.

## Week 1: look and learn

1. Pick the first case type for the test. Default: an O-1A case.
2. Make two or three completely invented cases, and gather a few public examples. Check what
   USCIS publishes and whether it can be used (public decisions only).
3. Study competitors only through what they publish: demo videos, help centres, docs, public
   sample outputs, reviews and Reddit threads. Do not sign up to trials that require being a
   lawyer, a firm email or being 18 (Ismael is 15 and not a lawyer; no fake identity). Run the
   same made up case through plain Claude and ChatGPT, since lawyers say those are the real
   competition, and save the outputs.
4. Build the smallest private demo: one intake in, one finished packet out (support letter,
   criteria mapping, exhibit index). Test it on the invented cases only.
5. Compare the demo with the plain Claude and ChatGPT outputs. Write down five things the demo does worse than a general Claude or ChatGPT chat. If there are
   more than two, stop and fix before showing anyone.

## Week 2: show it

1. Make a short captioned video of the demo on an invented case. Say it is a demo.
2. List 50 immigration lawyers and small firms who post in public. Read what each posts.
3. Send each a short message that shows you read their work and offers one thing: run a made up or
   public case through the tool and send the result in 24 hours. No questions about their pain,
   no request for files, no claims you cannot back.
4. Ask three of the people who reply if they would review the output. Offer free access.

## Week 3: decide

Count honestly:
* How many replied, how many asked for the demo, how many said the output was good, how many said
  they would pay, and what they said it should cost.

## Stop rule

If fewer than three lawyers say they would pay, or no lawyer will review the work, stop and
write down why. Then decide what to test next. Do not keep building to avoid the answer.

## If it goes well

Pick the name, set up a company with the parent or guardian, write a real privacy and data policy
(only true statements), sign the model provider data terms, and build the first ready made
workflows with the advisor lawyer reviewing.
